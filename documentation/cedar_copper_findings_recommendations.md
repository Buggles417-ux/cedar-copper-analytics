# Cedar & Copper Final Findings and Business Recommendations

## Executive Summary

Cedar & Copper generated **$4.00 million in net sales before refunds** from **30,000 orders** and **82,714 units** during 2024 and 2025. Recorded refunds reduced revenue by **$212,006**, producing **$3.79 million in refund-adjusted net revenue**. Merchandise performance remained financially strong, with **$2.32 million in net item margin** and a **57.92% margin rate** before refunds and non-merchandise costs.

The clearest business opportunities are to protect the High Value and Repeat customer segments, use Web as the primary scale channel while improving its basket value, preserve premium Cookware availability, and address the unusually high return activity concentrated in Seasonal Kitchen and selected Tabletop products. Leadership should also prepare inventory and fulfillment capacity for the sharp year-end demand increase visible in both years.

These conclusions describe patterns in the fictional dataset. They identify where leadership should investigate or test an action; they do not establish causation.

## Final Findings

### 1. Revenue growth is driven primarily by higher order volume, with a pronounced year-end peak

Net sales before refunds increased from **$1.42 million in 2024** to **$2.58 million in 2025**, an increase of approximately **81.7%**. Orders increased from **10,298** to **19,702**, or approximately **91.3%**, while average order value declined from **$137.87** to **$130.94**, a decrease of approximately **5.0%**.

December was the strongest month in both years. December 2025 produced **$433,310 from 3,337 orders**, compared with **$258,148 from 1,911 orders** in December 2024. November 2025 was the second-highest month at **$348,210**.

**Business interpretation:** The business expanded substantially, but order growth outpaced revenue growth. Leadership should view the 2025 increase as a volume-led result and monitor whether declining basket value continues. The November–December concentration also creates inventory, staffing, and fulfillment risk.

### 2. Web is the largest sales channel, while Store and Mobile App produce slightly larger orders

| Channel | Orders | Net sales before refunds | Revenue share | Average order value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Web | 13,429 | $1,764,633 | 44.1% | $131.40 |
| Store | 9,194 | $1,239,451 | 31.0% | $134.81 |
| Mobile App | 7,377 | $995,535 | 24.9% | $134.95 |

**Business interpretation:** Web is the company’s primary scale channel, accounting for more than two-fifths of sales. Mobile App and Store have similar and slightly higher average order values, but neither has Web’s order volume. The available data supports channel prioritization, but it does not explain why the differences exist.

### 3. Repeat and High Value customers account for most revenue, but they create different kinds of value

| Customer segment | Purchasing customers | Orders | Revenue | AOV | Orders per customer | Revenue per customer |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Repeat | 3,370 | 12,992 | $1,610,557 | $123.97 | 3.86 | $477.91 |
| High Value | 1,232 | 7,593 | $1,352,399 | $178.11 | 6.16 | $1,097.73 |
| Discount Driven | 1,444 | 4,162 | $467,222 | $112.26 | 2.88 | $323.56 |
| New | 2,194 | 4,214 | $440,483 | $104.53 | 1.92 | $200.77 |
| At Risk | 637 | 1,039 | $128,959 | $124.12 | 1.63 | $202.45 |

Repeat and High Value customers together generate approximately **74% of total sales**. Repeat customers provide the largest revenue pool, while High Value customers generate the strongest economics: the highest AOV, the highest purchase frequency, and **$1,097.73 in revenue per purchasing customer**, more than twice the Repeat segment’s level.

The purchase-frequency analysis also shows that **2,284 customers placed five or more orders**, the largest frequency group, while **1,123 of the 10,000 customer records had no order during the analysis period**.

**Business interpretation:** Cedar & Copper should not manage Repeat and High Value customers as one group. Repeat customers are the larger pool and present an upgrade opportunity; High Value customers are the smaller, more productive group whose retention has disproportionate importance.

### 4. Premium products drive revenue more than unit volume alone

The **Copper Core Cookware Set** is the top product by revenue at **$294,238**, with an average selling price of **$239.02 per unit**. The **Knife Block Set** ranks second at **$200,299**, with an average selling price of **$152.78**. Four of the top ten products by revenue are Cookware items.

By contrast, the **Linen Napkin Set** leads unit volume at **1,344 units** but produces relatively low revenue. This confirms that product revenue is influenced heavily by realized price and mix, not simply by unit volume.

**Business interpretation:** Unit rankings alone would understate the importance of premium Cookware and Kitchen Tools. Leadership should evaluate products using revenue, margin, unit volume, and return activity together.

### 5. Cookware is the largest revenue and margin contributor, while category tradeoffs matter

| Category | Revenue | Net item margin | Margin rate | Returned order-item rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Cookware | $1,041,522 | $556,520 | 53.4% | 4.6% |
| Tabletop | $663,360 | $392,477 | 59.2% | 8.1% |
| Kitchen Tools | $553,787 | $313,106 | 56.5% | 3.7% |
| Coffee & Tea | $438,538 | $261,742 | 59.7% | 5.7% |
| Food Storage | $415,404 | $245,396 | 59.1% | 5.0% |
| Bakeware | $310,743 | $187,425 | 60.3% | 4.2% |
| Seasonal Kitchen | $296,860 | $187,224 | 63.1% | 9.9% |
| Pantry Organization | $279,405 | $172,661 | 61.8% | 4.8% |

Cookware contributes the most revenue and the most margin dollars, despite having the lowest margin percentage among the categories. Seasonal Kitchen produces the highest margin percentage but the lowest sales among the categories and the highest return rate.

**Business interpretation:** Margin percentage should not be used by itself to make assortment decisions. Cookware’s lower percentage produces far more margin dollars because of its scale. Seasonal Kitchen’s attractive pre-refund margin rate must be weighed against its high return activity.

### 6. Return activity is concentrated enough to justify targeted investigation

The overall returned order-item rate is **5.65%**, based on **3,861 returned lines out of 68,292 order-item lines**. Seasonal Kitchen’s rate is **9.9%**, and Tabletop’s rate is **8.1%**.

The **Entertaining Cheese Board** has the highest product rate at **12.7%**, representing **81 returned lines out of 638**. Eight of the ten products with the highest rates are Seasonal Kitchen products. Other notable rates include Pumpkin Serving Platter at **11.4%**, Thanksgiving Pie Dish at **10.8%**, Outdoor Picnic Serve Set at **10.6%**, Holiday Mug Set at **10.1%**, Salad Plate Set at **9.1%**, and Flatware Set at **8.8%**.

Recorded refunds total **$212,006**, equal to approximately **5.3% of pre-refund sales**.

**Business interpretation:** This is a concentrated category and product issue rather than a uniform company-wide pattern. The data identifies where investigation should begin, but it does not prove that product quality is the cause. Product expectations, damage, packaging, fulfillment, seasonality, and other factors remain possible explanations.

## Business Recommendations

### Priority 1. Investigate and reduce Seasonal Kitchen and Tabletop returns

Begin with the highest-rate products, especially Entertaining Cheese Board, Pumpkin Serving Platter, Thanksgiving Pie Dish, Outdoor Picnic Serve Set, Holiday Mug Set, Salad Plate Set, and Flatware Set.

Leadership should:

1. Review return reasons by product and category.
2. Inspect product descriptions, images, dimensions, packaging, and fulfillment handling.
3. Compare return patterns by sales channel and time period to isolate operational or expectation-setting problems.
4. Establish a monthly product return watchlist showing returned lines, rate, sales volume, and refund dollars.

**Success measure:** Reduce Seasonal Kitchen’s returned order-item rate from **9.9%** toward the company baseline of **5.65%** without materially weakening sales or margin dollars.

### Priority 2. Protect High Value customers and move qualified Repeat customers upward

High Value customers generate **$1,097.73 per purchasing customer** and place **6.16 orders each**, making retention especially valuable. Repeat customers provide the largest revenue pool and are the natural audience for conversion into the High Value segment.

Leadership should:

1. Create retention monitoring for High Value customers, including declining purchase frequency and time since last purchase.
2. Identify Repeat customers approaching High Value behavior based on order frequency, revenue, and average order value.
3. Test targeted early access, replenishment reminders, bundles, or loyalty benefits rather than broad discounts.
4. Track movement among New, Repeat, High Value, and At Risk groups over time.

**Success measure:** Improve retention and revenue per purchasing customer while avoiding unnecessary discounting to customers already likely to purchase.

### Priority 3. Use Web for scale and improve its basket value

Web generates **44.1% of sales** but has the lowest channel AOV at **$131.40**, compared with approximately **$134.80–$134.95** for Store and Mobile App.

Leadership should:

1. Preserve Web acquisition and conversion strength.
2. Test product bundles, complementary-product recommendations, and threshold-based offers that encourage larger baskets.
3. Study Mobile App and Store purchase patterns for transferable merchandising or cross-sell practices.
4. Compare channel profitability after marketing and operating costs before shifting investment.

**Success measure:** Increase Web AOV without reducing conversion rate, margin percentage, or order volume.

### Priority 4. Protect premium-product availability and manage the assortment using multiple measures

Premium Cookware and Kitchen Tools are major revenue drivers. The Copper Core Cookware Set and Knife Block Set alone generate approximately **$494,537** in sales.

Leadership should:

1. Prioritize inventory availability for proven premium products before peak demand periods.
2. Use revenue, margin dollars, units, average selling price, and return rate together in assortment reviews.
3. Pair high-volume, lower-priced products with premium items in bundles or recommendations.
4. Avoid reducing support for Cookware merely because its **53.4% margin rate** is lower than other categories; it produces the most margin dollars.

**Success measure:** Maintain in-stock performance and premium-product revenue while preserving category margin dollars.

### Priority 5. Prepare earlier for the November–December demand surge

The two strongest months are November and December 2025, and December is the peak month in both years.

Leadership should:

1. Set inventory, staffing, and fulfillment plans before November.
2. Use category and product demand patterns to prioritize seasonal purchasing.
3. Monitor weekly revenue, orders, fulfillment performance, and returns during the peak period.
4. Review whether Q4 growth is profitable after refunds, promotional costs, and operational expense.

**Success measure:** Support peak-period order growth without increasing stockouts, late fulfillment, return activity, or margin erosion.

### Priority 6. Institutionalize the validated KPI definitions

Leadership should use the KPI dictionary and validation baselines as reporting controls. Dashboard owners should reconcile results after data refreshes and clearly distinguish **Net Sales Before Refunds** from **Refund-Adjusted Net Revenue**.

**Success measure:** No unexplained variance from source totals, consistent KPI labels across reporting, and documented approval for any future metric-definition change.

## Recommended Leadership Sequence

1. Launch the Seasonal Kitchen and Tabletop return investigation.
2. Establish High Value retention and Repeat-to-High-Value monitoring.
3. Test Web basket-building tactics.
4. Protect premium-product availability and prepare for Q4 demand.
5. Maintain monthly KPI reconciliation and data-quality review.

## Data Limitations

1. Cedar & Copper is a fictional business using synthetic data. The findings demonstrate an analytics process and should not be represented as real-company performance.
2. The analysis is descriptive. It identifies relationships and priorities but does not prove that a particular factor caused an outcome.
3. Net sales and net item margin are before refunds. Net item margin also excludes marketing, shipping, store, labor, overhead, and other operating costs, so it is not company profit.
4. Returned quantity is unavailable. The return KPI measures returned order-item lines, not returned units.
5. Refund timing may differ from order timing. Refund-adjusted revenue should preserve the documented date treatment.
6. Customer `first_order_date` is not consistently derivable from the 2024–2025 order table and should be treated as a historical source attribute.
7. Customer segment labels are supplied by the dataset. Their underlying assignment rules are not available, so segment movement should be operationalized only after the business defines reproducible criteria.
8. Marketing and channel recommendations should be tested before scaling because the current data does not provide a complete causal or fully loaded profitability view.

## Final Leadership Takeaway

Cedar & Copper’s strongest performance comes from high order growth, a scalable Web channel, valuable Repeat and High Value customers, and premium products that generate substantial revenue and margin dollars. The most immediate risk is concentrated return activity in Seasonal Kitchen and selected Tabletop products. Leadership can act on these findings by protecting its most productive customers and products, improving Web basket value, preparing for year-end demand, and investigating returns with product-level discipline.
